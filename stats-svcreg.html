How broadly is each AWS service deployed, as a fraction of all regions in the current snapshot?

How this is calculated
  • Coverage = regions where the service is available ÷ regions in the chosen basis, expressed as a percentage. Each row in the chart is one service; the blue bar shows that service's coverage against the current basis.
  • This is a theoretical measure: it assumes every service could be deployed in every region. In practice AWS does not deploy all services in all regions, and some services are intentionally regional or still rolling out. A service at < 100% is not necessarily "incomplete" in any operational sense.
  • The region basis selector switches the denominator between all regions, default (commercial) regions only, or opt-in regions only — this changes the percentages for every service, so compare like-for-like.
  • The category selector narrows the services shown by AWS service category; the search box filters by service name or slug.
  • The coverage range selectors let you slice to a band (e.g. 20–50%) in 10% steps. Useful for finding services that are partially rolled out.
  • A service showing 0% under a given basis simply means it is not deployed in any region of that class in the current snapshot — for example, a service that only lives in opt-in regions will show 0% under Default only. Switch the basis to confirm. These rows are drawn with no bar rather than a tiny misleading nub.
What this is not
  1. Service availability is based on AWS-provided data. A service being available in a region does not mean that all sub-services, features, or sub-features of that service are available in the region. Feature-level parity varies by service and is not captured on this page — the bar only tells you the service name appears in that region's SSM inventory.
  2. Coverage is not a quality or maturity score. A high percentage does not imply a service is "better" or more mature; a low percentage does not imply it is "worse" or newer. Some services are intentionally scoped to a subset of regions (regional-only services, regulated-industry services, early-launch rollouts, geo-restricted features).
  3. Coverage is not a demand signal. A service in only a few regions does not mean customers want it in more; a service in all regions does not mean it is heavily used in all of them. Rollout is driven by AWS planning, not by this page.
  4. Only the AWS commercial partition is covered. GovCloud, China, European sovereign cloud, and isolated regions are excluded from both the service list and the region basis.
